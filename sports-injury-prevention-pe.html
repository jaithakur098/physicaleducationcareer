Sports injury prevention in school PE

Most school injuries are not dramatic fractures. They are ankles on uneven grounds, fingers in a poorly caught ball, and heat illness in a long afternoon final. Prevention is organisation.

This is general educational material. It is not medical advice. Serious injury needs a qualified medical professional.

Before the lesson

  • Walk the ground: holes, glass, wet patches, unprotected posts.
  • Footwear: no sliding sandals for running games.
  • Jewellery off; nails trimmed for catching games.
  • Know who has asthma or a recent injury; they sit out or do a modified task.

Warm-up that matches the sport

A 100 m race needs strides and starts. A throwing lesson needs shoulder circles and light standing throws. Static stretching of cold muscles as the only warm-up is a poor match for sprinting.

Matching and overuse

Do not put an untrained Class 6 student in a full-contact senior football game “to make up numbers”. Limit throwing volume in a single period. Rotate pitchers / servers.

If an injury happens

  • Stop the activity. Crowd control.
  • Do not relocate a deformity.
  • For a mild sprain, RICE is a first-aid memory aid: Rest, Ice (not on bare skin for long), Compression if trained, Elevation. Reassess.
  • Inform the office and parents as per school rules.
