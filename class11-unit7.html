Unit 7 – Fundamentals of Anatomy, Physiology and Kinesiology in Sports

Complete CBSE Notes, Human Body Systems, Movement Science and Sports Performance.

Introduction

Anatomy, Physiology and Kinesiology are important branches of sports science. These subjects help us understand the structure, functions and movement of the human body. Knowledge of these areas helps athletes, coaches and physical education teachers improve sports performance and prevent injuries.

Important: Anatomy studies body structure, Physiology studies body functions and Kinesiology studies movement.

Meaning of Anatomy

Anatomy is the branch of science that studies the structure of the human body. It deals with bones, muscles, joints, organs and body systems.

Knowledge of anatomy helps in understanding body composition and movement during sports activities.

Meaning of Physiology

Physiology is the study of the functions of different organs and systems of the human body. It explains how the heart, lungs, muscles and other organs work during exercise and sports activities.

Meaning of Kinesiology

Kinesiology is the scientific study of human movement. It focuses on how muscles, bones and joints work together to produce movement.

Kinesiology helps improve sports techniques and prevent injuries.

Importance of Anatomy, Physiology and Kinesiology in Sports

  • Improves sports performance.
  • Helps prevent injuries.
  • Improves training methods.
  • Enhances fitness development.
  • Supports scientific coaching.
  • Helps understand body movements.
  • Improves rehabilitation after injury.
  • Enhances athletic efficiency.

Skeletal System

The skeletal system forms the framework of the human body. It consists of bones, joints and cartilage. An adult human body contains approximately 206 bones.

Functions of Skeletal System

  • Provides support.
  • Protects internal organs.
  • Helps movement.
  • Stores minerals.
  • Produces blood cells.

Muscular System

The muscular system consists of muscles that help in body movement, posture and stability. Muscles work together with bones and joints to perform various physical activities.

Types of Muscles

  • Skeletal Muscles: Attached to bones and responsible for voluntary movements.
  • Smooth Muscles: Found in internal organs and function involuntarily.
  • Cardiac Muscles: Found only in the heart.

Functions of Muscular System

  • Produces body movement.
  • Maintains posture.
  • Provides body stability.
  • Generates heat.
  • Supports joints.
  • Improves athletic performance.

Joints and Their Types

A joint is the point where two or more bones meet. Joints enable movement and flexibility in the body.

Types of Joints

  • Ball and Socket Joint: Shoulder, Hip
  • Hinge Joint: Elbow, Knee
  • Pivot Joint: Neck
  • Gliding Joint: Wrist, Ankle
  • Fixed Joint: Skull Bones

Movement at Joints

  • Flexion
  • Extension
  • Abduction
  • Adduction
  • Rotation
  • Circumduction

These movements are important for sports performance and daily physical activities.

Role of Physiology in Sports

Physiology helps us understand how body systems respond during exercise and sports activities. It explains energy production, muscle contraction, heart function and respiratory efficiency.

  • Improves training methods.
  • Enhances endurance.
  • Helps monitor fitness levels.
  • Improves recovery.
  • Supports scientific coaching.

Role of Kinesiology in Sports

Kinesiology helps athletes perform movements efficiently. It analyzes body mechanics and movement patterns to improve performance and reduce injury risk.

  • Improves technique.
  • Enhances movement efficiency.
  • Reduces injury risk.
  • Supports rehabilitation.
  • Improves sports performance.

Important Examination Points

  • Anatomy studies body structure.
  • Physiology studies body functions.

  • The human body contains 206 bones.
  • Muscles help movement and posture.

  • Ball and Socket Joint allows maximum movement.
  • Kinesiology improves sports technique.
