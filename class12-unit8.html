Unit 8: Biomechanics and Sports

Biomechanics is the study of movement and the application of mechanical principles to human motion. In sports, biomechanics helps athletes improve performance, efficiency and safety by understanding how the body moves during physical activities.

Meaning of Biomechanics

Biomechanics is a branch of sports science that studies the forces acting on the human body and the effects produced by these forces during movement.

Importance of Biomechanics in Sports

  • Improves sports performance.
  • Enhances movement efficiency.
  • Reduces risk of injury.
  • Improves technique.
  • Helps coaches analyze performance.

Meaning of Sports Biomechanics

Sports biomechanics applies the principles of mechanics to sports movements such as running, jumping, throwing and swimming to improve performance.

Newton's Laws of Motion

First Law of Motion (Law of Inertia)

An object remains at rest or continues moving with uniform velocity unless acted upon by an external force.

Examples in Sports

  • A football remains stationary until kicked.
  • A cricket ball continues moving until stopped.

Second Law of Motion (Law of Acceleration)

The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the force applied and inversely proportional to its mass.

Examples in Sports

  • A harder kick sends a football farther.
  • A stronger throw increases ball speed.

Third Law of Motion (Law of Action and Reaction)

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Examples in Sports

  • Running and sprinting.
  • Swimming strokes.
  • Jumping activities.

Types of Motion

Linear Motion

Movement in a straight line from one point to another.

Examples

  • 100-meter sprint.
  • Swimming in a straight lane.

Angular Motion

Movement around an axis.

Examples

  • Gymnastics rotations.
  • Cycling pedal movement.

General Motion

Combination of linear and angular motion.

Examples

  • Running.
  • Long jump.
  • High jump.

Projectile Motion

Projectile motion occurs when an object is thrown into the air and moves under the influence of gravity.

Factors Affecting Projectile Motion

  • Angle of release.
  • Velocity of release.
  • Height of release.
  • Air resistance.

Examples

  • Shot Put.
  • Javelin Throw.
  • Basketball Shooting.

Friction in Sports

Friction is the force that opposes motion between two surfaces in contact.

Advantages of Friction

  • Provides grip during running.
  • Helps athletes maintain balance.
  • Improves performance in sports shoes.

Disadvantages of Friction

  • Causes wear and tear.
  • Reduces speed in some activities.
  • Produces heat.

Balance in Sports

Balance is the ability to maintain the body's center of gravity over its base of support.

Factors Affecting Balance

  • Size of base of support.
  • Position of center of gravity.
  • Body mass.
  • Friction.

Force

Force is a push or pull that changes the state of motion of an object.

Effects of Force

  • Starts motion.
  • Stops motion.
  • Changes direction.
  • Changes speed.

Levers in Human Body

A lever is a rigid bar that rotates around a fixed point called the fulcrum.

First Class Lever

Fulcrum lies between effort and resistance.

Example

  • Neck movement.

Second Class Lever

Resistance lies between fulcrum and effort.

Example

  • Standing on toes.

Third Class Lever

Effort lies between fulcrum and resistance.

Example

  • Biceps curl.

Application of Biomechanics in Sports

  • Improves technique.
  • Enhances performance.
  • Reduces injuries.
  • Improves training methods.
  • Helps in equipment design.

Conclusion

Biomechanics plays an important role in sports by helping athletes understand movement and improve performance. Knowledge of forces, motion, balance and levers helps athletes perform efficiently and safely.
