Unit 8: Biomechanics and Sports

Biomechanics is the study of movement and the application of mechanical principles to the human body. It helps athletes improve performance, technique and efficiency while reducing the risk of injury.

Meaning of Biomechanics

Biomechanics is a branch of sports science that studies human movement using the laws of mechanics. It examines how muscles, bones and joints work together during physical activities and sports performance.

Importance of Biomechanics in Sports

  • Improves sports performance.
  • Enhances movement efficiency.
  • Reduces risk of injury.
  • Improves technique.
  • Helps coaches analyze performance.

Newton's Laws of Motion

First Law of Motion

The First Law states that an object remains at rest or continues moving in a straight line unless acted upon by an external force.

Example: A football remains stationary until kicked by a player.

Second Law of Motion

The Second Law states that force is equal to mass multiplied by acceleration. Greater force produces greater acceleration.

Example: A stronger throw results in greater acceleration of a javelin.

Third Law of Motion

The Third Law states that for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Example: While running, the foot pushes backward against the ground and the body moves forward.

Equilibrium

Equilibrium is the state of balance in which all forces acting on the body are balanced. Good balance is essential for successful sports performance.

Types of Equilibrium

  • Static Equilibrium
  • Dynamic Equilibrium

Static Equilibrium

Static equilibrium occurs when the body remains balanced while at rest.

Dynamic Equilibrium

Dynamic equilibrium occurs when the body maintains balance while moving.

Center of Gravity

The center of gravity is the point where the entire weight of the body is considered to be concentrated. Lowering the center of gravity improves stability and balance.

Factors Affecting Stability

  • Size of the base of support.
  • Height of the center of gravity.
  • Body weight.
  • Friction.

Friction

Friction is the force that opposes motion between two surfaces in contact. In sports, friction plays an important role in maintaining balance and controlling movement.

Advantages of Friction

  • Provides better grip.
  • Helps maintain balance.
  • Improves performance in running and jumping.
  • Reduces slipping.

Disadvantages of Friction

  • Causes wear and tear.
  • Produces heat.
  • Reduces speed in some sports.

Projectile Motion

Projectile motion refers to the motion of an object that is thrown into the air and moves under the influence of gravity.

Examples include javelin throw, shot put, discus throw and basketball shooting.

Factors Affecting Projectile Motion

  • Angle of projection.
  • Velocity of projection.
  • Height of release.
  • Air resistance.
  • Gravity.

Force

Force is a push or pull that changes or tends to change the state of motion of an object.

In sports, force is essential for running, jumping, throwing and lifting.

Lever

A lever is a rigid bar that rotates around a fixed point called the fulcrum. Levers help the body perform movements efficiently.

Parts of a Lever

  • Fulcrum
  • Effort
  • Resistance (Load)

Types of Levers

First Class Lever

The fulcrum lies between the effort and resistance.

Example: Neck movement.

Second Class Lever

The resistance lies between the fulcrum and effort.

Example: Standing on toes.

Third Class Lever

The effort lies between the fulcrum and resistance.

Example: Biceps curl.

Angular Motion

Angular motion occurs when a body rotates around an axis. Gymnastics, diving and figure skating involve angular motion.

Linear Motion

Linear motion occurs when a body moves in a straight path from one point to another.

Example: Sprinting in a straight line.

Importance of Biomechanics in Sports

  • Improves sports techniques.
  • Enhances performance.
  • Prevents injuries.
  • Improves coaching methods.
  • Increases efficiency of movement.
