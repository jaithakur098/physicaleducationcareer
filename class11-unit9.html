Unit 9 – Training and Doping in Sports

Complete CBSE Notes on Sports Training, Training Principles and Doping in Sports.

Introduction to Sports Training

Sports training is a systematic and scientific process designed to improve physical fitness, sports skills, performance and overall athletic ability. It involves planned exercises, proper recovery and continuous evaluation.

The primary objective of sports training is to prepare athletes physically, mentally and technically for competition. Effective training helps athletes achieve maximum performance.

Important: Sports training is a planned process that improves performance through regular practice and scientific methods.

Meaning of Sports Training

Sports training refers to a systematic programme of physical exercises and activities aimed at improving athletic performance. It develops strength, endurance, speed, flexibility, coordination and sports-specific skills.

Training helps athletes adapt to increasing workloads and perform efficiently during competitions.

Aims of Sports Training

  • Improve physical fitness.
  • Develop sports skills.
  • Increase endurance.
  • Improve strength and speed.
  • Enhance flexibility.
  • Improve coordination.
  • Achieve peak performance.
  • Prepare athletes for competition.

Principles of Sports Training

The principles of training provide guidelines for designing effective training programmes.

1. Principle of Overload

The body must be challenged with greater workloads than normal to improve fitness and performance.

2. Principle of Progression

Training load should increase gradually over time to avoid injury and ensure continuous improvement.

3. Principle of Specificity

Training should be specific to the sport and skills being developed.

4. Principle of Recovery

Adequate rest and recovery are essential for adaptation and performance improvement.

5. Principle of Individual Differences

Training programmes should be designed according to individual needs and abilities.

Methods of Sports Training

  • Continuous Training Method
  • Interval Training Method
  • Circuit Training Method
  • Fartlek Training Method
  • Weight Training Method

Continuous Training Method

Continuous training involves performing exercise continuously without rest for a long duration at a moderate intensity.

It is mainly used to improve cardiovascular endurance and aerobic fitness.

Advantages

  • Improves endurance.
  • Enhances heart efficiency.
  • Increases stamina.
  • Improves aerobic capacity.

Interval Training Method

Interval training consists of repeated bouts of exercise separated by periods of rest or recovery.

It helps improve both aerobic and anaerobic fitness and is widely used by athletes.

Benefits

  • Improves speed.
  • Improves endurance.
  • Enhances recovery ability.
  • Increases performance capacity.

Doping in Sports

Doping refers to the use of prohibited substances or methods by athletes to improve performance artificially. Doping is considered unethical and is strictly prohibited in sports competitions worldwide.

Athletes who use doping substances gain an unfair advantage over competitors and may face severe penalties if detected.

Important: Doping violates the spirit of fair play and can seriously harm an athlete's health.

Reasons for Doping

  • Desire to win competitions.
  • Pressure from coaches or supporters.
  • Financial rewards.
  • Fame and recognition.
  • Lack of awareness.
  • Competitive pressure.

Types of Doping

1. Stimulants

Stimulants increase alertness, energy and concentration. They may temporarily improve performance but can be harmful to health.

2. Anabolic Agents

These substances increase muscle size and strength artificially.

3. Narcotics

Used to reduce pain and allow athletes to continue performing despite injuries.

4. Diuretics

Used to reduce body weight rapidly or hide the presence of other prohibited substances.

5. Blood Doping

A method used to increase the oxygen-carrying capacity of blood and improve endurance performance.

Effects of Doping on Health

  • Heart diseases.
  • High blood pressure.
  • Liver damage.
  • Kidney disorders.
  • Hormonal imbalance.
  • Mental health problems.
  • Addiction.
  • Risk of sudden death.

Effects of Doping on Sports Career

  • Disqualification from competitions.
  • Suspension from sports.
  • Loss of medals and awards.
  • Damage to reputation.
  • Loss of sponsorship opportunities.
  • Permanent ban in serious cases.

WADA (World Anti-Doping Agency)

The World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) was established in 1999 to promote and coordinate the fight against doping in sports.

Functions of WADA

  • Creates anti-doping rules.
  • Maintains prohibited substance lists.
  • Conducts testing programmes.
  • Promotes clean sports.
  • Educates athletes about doping risks.

Prevention of Doping

  • Education and awareness.
  • Regular anti-doping testing.
  • Ethical sports culture.
  • Scientific training methods.
  • Proper nutrition and recovery.
  • Guidance from qualified coaches.

Important Examination Points

  • Sports training is a systematic process.
  • Overload principle improves fitness.
  • Progression should be gradual.
  • Recovery is essential for performance.
  • Doping is prohibited in sports.

  • Anabolic agents increase muscle mass.
  • Doping can damage health and career.
