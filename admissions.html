Which Physical Education course fits you?

The most common mistake in this field is applying for the wrong course, then discovering after two years that it does not meet the eligibility rules for the job you actually want. Work backwards from the post you are aiming at.

  • Just finished Class 12 and want a full degree: B.P.E.S (Bachelor of Physical Education and Sports), a three or four year undergraduate degree.
  • Just finished Class 12 and want the shortest teaching route: D.P.Ed (Diploma in Physical Education), a two-year diploma used for primary and upper primary level posts in several states.
  • Already a graduate and want to teach in secondary school: B.P.Ed, a two-year professional degree. This is the standard qualification for a school PTI or PET post.
  • Already hold a B.P.Ed and want senior secondary or college teaching: M.P.Ed, a two-year postgraduate degree.
  • Hold an M.P.Ed and want college teaching or research: Ph.D, with UGC NET where required.

The five checks to run on any college before paying

  • NCTE recognition for that exact programme and session. B.P.Ed, M.P.Ed and D.P.Ed are teacher education programmes governed by NCTE. Ask for the recognition order, check the programme name and the sanctioned intake, and cross-check on the NCTE website. An unrecognised degree is regularly rejected at document verification in government recruitment.
  • University recognition. Confirm UGC recognition and, for private universities, establishment under a state Act.
  • Written fee structure. Tuition, examination, hostel, mess, uniform and one-time charges, all on official letterhead.
  • Faculty and infrastructure. A genuine department has qualified faculty, a track or field, courts, a gymnasium and a laboratory.
  • Admission process. Ask whether there is an entrance test, a physical efficiency test, sports certificate weightage, and what last year's cut-off was.

Documents to keep ready

  • Class 10 and Class 12 marksheets and certificates
  • Graduation marksheets and degree or provisional certificate, where applicable
  • Transfer certificate and migration certificate
  • Category certificate, if applicable, in the prescribed format
  • Domicile certificate, where the state requires it
  • Sports participation certificates at school, district, state or national level
  • Medical fitness certificate, photographs and identity proof

Preparing for the physical efficiency test

Most B.P.Ed and D.P.Ed admissions include a physical fitness or efficiency test. Standards differ between universities, but the events are usually similar: a sprint, an endurance run, a jump event, a throw event and sometimes push-ups or sit-ups. Begin training at least three months before the test, work on endurance and technique together, and never attempt to peak in the final fortnight. Sports certificates often carry weightage in the merit list, so collect and preserve them.
