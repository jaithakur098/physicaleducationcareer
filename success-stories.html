About these accounts

The accounts below are written from the experiences students and parents have shared with us by phone, email and WhatsApp during admission counselling and board preparation. Names have been shortened or changed at the students' request, and no photographs are published. They are shared to show what a realistic preparation routine looks like — not as a guarantee of results. Outcomes depend on each student's own effort, and on eligibility and selection decisions taken entirely by boards, universities and recruiting authorities.

What actually made the difference

Across every account we have collected, the same few habits come up repeatedly. None of them is dramatic, and all of them are available to any student for free.

  • Writing answers by hand instead of reading them. Almost every student who improved sharply started attempting full papers against a clock rather than re-reading notes.
  • Targeting the high-weightage units first. Fixtures, postural deformities, yoga for lifestyle diseases and nutrition carry a disproportionate share of the marks.
  • Practising diagrams. Labelled diagrams are among the fastest marks in the paper and among the most commonly lost.
  • Taking the practical file seriously. The practical component is 30 marks and is entirely within the student's control.
  • Checking NCTE recognition before choosing a college. Students who verified approvals early avoided the single most expensive mistake in this field.
  • Maintaining fitness year-round. Candidates who kept training passed physical efficiency tests comfortably; those who started a month before did not.
