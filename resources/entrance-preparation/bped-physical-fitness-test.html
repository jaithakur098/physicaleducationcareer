B.P.Ed physical fitness test: train the battery, not a rumour

Universities publish their own physical fitness test (PFT). Some use a 50 m sprint, standing broad jump, shot put, and a 600 m or 800 m run. Others add sit-ups, pull-ups, or a game skill. Print the current prospectus. The table below is a training template, not a promise of marks.

Do not start a high-intensity programme if you have chest pain, uncontrolled asthma, recent surgery or a joint injury — get medical clearance. This page is educational, not a prescription.

How to read the official battery

  1. List every item and whether it is scored or only qualifying.
  2. Note the surface (cinder, synthetic, grass) — times change with surface.
  3. Note the implement weight for throws (men/women often differ).
  4. Note the order of events. If endurance is last, do not empty the tank in the sprint warm-up.

Typical components and what they measure

Item (examples)QualityTraining idea (general)
30–50 m sprintAcceleration2 sessions/week of 4–6 flying or standing starts; full rest.
Standing broad jumpPowerSquat pattern + 3×5 standing jumps; land softly.
Shot put / medicine ball throwPower, coordinationTechnique first; light implement before heavy.
600–1000 m runAnaerobic / aerobic mixOne interval session + one easy run per week.
Sit-ups / push-upsMuscular enduranceEvery other day, stop 2 reps before form collapse.

Eight-week outline (if you already play a sport)

  • Weeks 1–2: technique of jump and throw; easy runs 20–25 minutes; sprint drills without max speed every day.
  • Weeks 3–5: add measured attempts once a week on the same ground you will be tested on if possible.
  • Weeks 6–7: simulate the full order of the university battery once per week.
  • Week 8: taper — shorter sessions, same quality, extra sleep.
