Class 12 Unit 1 — Management of sporting events

Meaning and functions of sports management

Sports management is the process of planning, organising, staffing, directing, controlling and evaluating every activity connected with a sporting event or a sports organisation. In a school or college setting it covers everything from framing the annual sports calendar to arranging equipment, forming committees, drawing fixtures and publishing results. Good management is what allows a competition to run safely and fairly; poor management is the single most common reason school tournaments collapse.

  • Planning: deciding the objectives, the date, the venue, the budget and the events to be conducted.
  • Organising: arranging equipment, grounds, officials and finances so the plan can be executed.
  • Staffing: appointing the right people to the right committee according to competence.
  • Directing: guiding and supervising officials and volunteers during the event.
  • Controlling: comparing what is happening with what was planned and correcting deviations.
  • Evaluating: reviewing the event afterwards to improve the next one.

Committees and their responsibilities

Committees are formed before, during and after a tournament. Pre-event committees handle publicity, entries, finance, grounds and equipment. During the event, the committees for officials, first aid, refreshments and the announcement of results take over. After the event, the committees for prize distribution, accounts and reporting complete the work.

  • Publicity committee: circulars, invitations, notices and media information.
  • Committee for grounds and equipment: marking the field, checking the equipment and ensuring safety.
  • Committee for officials: appointing referees, umpires, judges, scorers and timekeepers.
  • Finance committee: preparing the budget, collecting entry fees and maintaining accounts.
  • Committee for refreshment and boarding: water, refreshments and accommodation for visiting teams.
  • First aid committee: a stocked first aid kit, a trained attendant and an ambulance arrangement.
  • Committee for the announcement of results: compiling scores and declaring results accurately.

Tournaments and their types

A tournament is a series of contests conducted to determine a winner. The two basic forms are the knock-out tournament and the league or round-robin tournament, and combination forms are built from these two.

  • Knock-out (elimination) tournament: the losing team is eliminated immediately. It is economical, takes the least time and needs the fewest officials, but a strong team eliminated early gets no second chance and the result can be unfair.
  • League or round-robin tournament: every team plays every other team. The result is the most reliable measure of ability, but it needs far more time, money, grounds and officials.
  • Combination tournament: knock-out cum league, league cum knock-out, and similar formats used to balance fairness against time.
  • Challenge tournament: ladder and pyramid formats, used mostly in individual sports within an institution.

Fixtures, byes and seeding

A fixture is the programme that shows which team plays which, and when. Drawing fixtures correctly is the most frequently examined part of this unit.

  • Number of matches in a knock-out tournament: total teams minus one.
  • Number of matches in a league tournament: N multiplied by (N minus 1), divided by 2, where N is the number of teams.
  • Byes: when the number of teams is not a power of two, byes are given so that the second round has a number of teams that is a power of two. The number of byes equals the next higher power of two minus the number of teams. Byes are distributed first to the last team of the lower half, then the first team of the upper half, then the last team of the upper half, then the first team of the lower half.
  • Seeding: placing strong teams in the draw deliberately so that they do not meet in the early rounds. Special seeding is used when the organisers already know the relative strength of the teams.
  • Cyclic method and staircase method: two standard methods of drawing league fixtures. In the cyclic method one team is fixed and the others rotate; the staircase method needs no consideration of byes at all.

Intramural and extramural competitions

Intramural comes from intra meaning within and muros meaning walls: these are competitions conducted within the walls of one institution, among its own students. Their aim is mass participation, health, recreation, discipline and the identification of talent. Extramural competitions are held outside the institution, between different institutions, and aim at higher standards of performance, exposure and sportsmanship.

Community sports and specific sports programmes

Community sports programmes bring physical activity to people outside formal education — run for fun events, sports days, health awareness rallies and yoga camps. Specific sports programmes include events such as Sports Day, Health Run, Run for Unity, Run for Fun and Run for a Specific Cause, each of which combines participation with a social message.

MCQs for practice

  1. Number of matches in a knock-out tournament of 13 teams is: (a) 12 (b) 13 (c) 14 (d) 26. Answer: (a) 12 because matches = N − 1.
  2. A bye is given when the number of teams is: (a) odd only (b) not a power of two (c) more than 16 (d) even. Answer: (b).
  3. Intramural competitions are held: (a) between two schools (b) within one institution (c) only at state level (d) only for professionals. Answer: (b).

Short-answer questions

  • State two advantages and two disadvantages of a knock-out tournament.
  • Write the formula for matches in a single league of N teams.
  • Name four pre-event committees.

Long-answer questions

  • Draw a knock-out fixture for 11 teams. Show byes and the first-round matches.
  • Explain seeding. Why do organisers use it?

Common mistakes

  • Writing “matches = N + 1” for knock-out.
  • Placing all byes in one half of the draw.
  • Calling a league “knock-out cum league” without explaining both stages.

Revision points

  • Knock-out matches = N − 1; league matches = N(N − 1)/2.
  • Byes = next power of 2 minus N.
  • Intramural = within walls; extramural = between institutions.
